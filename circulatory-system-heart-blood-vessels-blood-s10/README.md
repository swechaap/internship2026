# Blood Circulation Process (S10)

## Introduction
Blood circulation is the continuous movement of blood through the heart, lungs, and body.  
It helps deliver oxygen and nutrients to cells and removes carbon dioxide and other wastes.

## Oxygen-Rich Circulation
Oxygen-rich blood (blood with oxygen) comes from the lungs and follows this path:
1. It enters the **left atrium** of the heart.
2. It moves into the **left ventricle**.
3. The left ventricle pumps it through the **aorta**.
4. From the aorta, it reaches all parts of the body.

## Oxygen-Poor Circulation
After body cells use oxygen, blood becomes oxygen-poor and follows this path:
1. It returns from body tissues to the heart.
2. It enters the **right atrium**.
3. It moves into the **right ventricle**.
4. The right ventricle pumps it to the lungs through the **pulmonary artery**.
5. In the lungs, blood receives oxygen again.

## Step-by-Step Blood Circulation Process
1. Body organs need oxygen to work.
2. In the lungs, oxygen enters the blood.
3. Oxygen-rich blood travels to the left side of the heart.
4. The heart pumps oxygen-rich blood to all body organs.
5. Organs use oxygen and produce carbon dioxide.
6. Oxygen-poor blood returns to the right side of the heart.
7. The heart sends oxygen-poor blood to the lungs.
8. In the lungs, blood gives out carbon dioxide and picks up oxygen again.

## Storyboard (6 Frames)

### Frame 1: Body Needs Oxygen
- **Description:** A student is running and body cells need oxygen for energy.
- **Visual elements:** Human body outline, active organs (brain, muscles), oxygen icons decreasing.
- **Learning objective:** Understand that body tissues constantly need oxygen.

### Frame 2: Oxygen Enters Blood in Lungs
- **Description:** Air enters the lungs and oxygen moves into blood in tiny air sacs.
- **Visual elements:** Lungs, alveoli, arrows showing oxygen entering blood.
- **Learning objective:** Learn that lungs are the place where blood receives oxygen.

### Frame 3: Oxygen-Rich Blood Reaches Heart
- **Description:** Oxygen-rich blood travels from lungs to the left atrium, then to the left ventricle.
- **Visual elements:** Heart diagram with highlighted left atrium and left ventricle, red arrows from lungs.
- **Learning objective:** Identify the left side of the heart in oxygen-rich blood flow.

### Frame 4: Heart Pumps Blood to Body
- **Description:** Left ventricle pumps oxygen-rich blood through the aorta to all organs.
- **Visual elements:** Heart, aorta, branching arteries to brain, arms, stomach, and legs.
- **Learning objective:** Understand the pumping role of the left ventricle and aorta.

### Frame 5: Organs Use Oxygen
- **Description:** Body organs use oxygen and release carbon dioxide into blood.
- **Visual elements:** Organ cells taking oxygen (O₂) and releasing carbon dioxide (CO₂), color change in blood path.
- **Learning objective:** Explain why blood becomes oxygen-poor after passing through tissues.

### Frame 6: Oxygen-Poor Blood Returns to Heart and Lungs
- **Description:** Oxygen-poor blood returns to right atrium, moves to right ventricle, and is pumped to lungs for oxygenation.
- **Visual elements:** Blue arrows from body to right heart and pulmonary artery to lungs.
- **Learning objective:** Trace oxygen-poor blood flow back to lungs for re-oxygenation.

## Conclusion
Blood circulation is a repeating cycle that keeps us alive.  
The left side of the heart sends oxygen-rich blood to the body, and the right side sends oxygen-poor blood to the lungs.  
This cycle ensures every cell gets oxygen and waste gases are removed.
